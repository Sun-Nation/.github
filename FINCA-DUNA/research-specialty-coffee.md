# Specialty Coffee (Anchor Product) — Temporary Slide Brief

**Why this matters:** The price an investor can believe Finca Duna will get for a pound of green coffee is the single biggest input to the revenue model. This brief moves us from "Colombian coffee" to a *defensible* per-pound assumption for a high-altitude, Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta (SNSM) specialty farm.

---

## CORE QUESTION
What FOB price per pound can a new, quality-focused SNSM coffee farm realistically expect for its green coffee, and what market structure / regulation must it plan around to capture that price?

## KEY SUB-QUESTIONS
1. How much premium do Colombian washed Arabicas ("Colombian Milds") carry over the global commodity composite?
2. Does the SNSM / Magdalena origin add credibility (cup profile, altitude, reputation)?
3. Where does Colombian specialty coffee sell, and through which channels (commodity vs. direct trade)?
4. What do transparent roasters/importers actually *pay* for comparable high-score lots?
5. What does EUDR require of a brand-new farm that wants to export to the EU?
6. What single defensible FOB price range should the revenue model use?

---

## DATAPOINTS GATHERED

### 1. Colombian Milds premium over the composite I-CIP — **CONFIRMED**
ICO publishes a "Colombian Milds" group indicator (washed Arabica: Colombia, Kenya, Tanzania) vs. the composite I-CIP. Recent monthly values (US cents/lb):

| Month 2025 | Colombian Milds | I-CIP composite | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | ~351.9 | ~310.1 | ~+42 |
| Aug | ~366.7 | ~297.1 | ~+70 |
| Sep | ~403.8 | ~324.6 | ~+79 |
| Oct | ~403.3 | ~326.4 | ~+77 |

So Colombian washed Arabica is fetching roughly **+40 to +80 US cents/lb over the global composite** in 2025, a premium that *widened* through the year. (Source: ICO Coffee Market Reports / Indicator Price sheets.)
*Note: this is the commodity-grade Colombian premium, NOT the specialty premium — specialty sits well above this.*

### 2. SNSM / Magdalena origin — **CONFIRMED**
- **Denomination of origin** "Café de la Sierra Nevada" granted by Colombia's SIC on 30 Jan 2017 (Resolution 2484): officially a "clean and balanced cup, medium-high uniform body, medium acidity, chocolate flavors."
- **Altitude:** specialty lots commonly 1,200–1,800 m; the DO region spans ~500–1,900 m. Finca Duna's high-altitude positioning is consistent with the upper specialty band.
- **Cup profile / reputation:** smooth, chocolate-forward, cocoa depth, fuller body, cleaner finish, lower/medium acidity — "rounded and approachable" vs. the brighter central-Andes profile. Reputation is for *reliable, clean, balanced, consistent* specialty.
- **Sustainability story (investor-relevant):** ~93% shade-grown under native trees; cultivated largely by Arhuaco/indigenous and campesino communities (Arhuaco ~70% of growers in parts of the region). This supports a regenerative + organic + ethical narrative.
- Caveat: SNSM's *baseline* reputation is "consistent and clean," not "exotic 90+." Premium positioning must be earned with processing/variety, not assumed from origin alone.

### 3. Export markets & channels — **CONFIRMED**
- **Top destinations (MY 2024/25):** USA >40% share (~5.3M bags), then EU, Canada, Japan; USA+EU+Canada ≈ **75% of total trade**. China rose from #18 (2019) to ~#6 (2023) — fastest-growing market.
- **Channel split:** Fedecafé (Federation) handles ~18% of exports; private exporters ~82%. Specialty is now **>40% of Colombia's output** and growing via micro-lots, direct trade, and novel processing (co-ferments).
- **Path to market for Finca Duna:** either (a) sell green through an exporter/importer into the commodity-plus channel, or (b) build *direct-trade relationships* with roasters who publish transparency data (higher price, but requires reputation, volume reliability, and traceability).
- US tariff note: coffee was *exempted* from US reciprocal tariffs on 14 Nov 2025 — a tailwind for the largest market.

### 4. Buyer/roaster benchmarks (what transparent buyers actually pay) — **CONFIRMED (point figures)**
- **Onyx Coffee Lab** (publishes FOB/EXW + cup score per lot): recent Colombian examples ~**$3.90–$4.19/lb EXW** (Inza Blend, Aponte Village).
- **Tim Wendelboe:** average green price paid **$5.93/lb (2018)** and **$5.96/lb (2022)** — top-tier direct-trade ceiling.
- **Nomad Coffee (2023):** 52 lots, avg cup score **87 pts**; Colombia was its #1 origin; publishes both FOB *and* farmgate. (Individual lot $/kg not captured here — see open unknowns.)
- **Specialty Coffee Transaction Guide (2024 edition, ~105k contracts):**
  - Overall 3-yr median FOB **$3.48/lb** (+$0.73 vs prior 3 yrs).
  - 80–83.9 pts: median **$2.93/lb**.
  - 84+ ("fancy"): median **$4.18/lb**.
  - **86–87.9 pts: median ~$4.38/lb.**
  - 88+ increasing fastest of all bands.
- Reality check: FOB is the at-port price; farmgate (what the producer nets) is lower, and roaster landed cost is higher. As a *producer*, Finca Duna's realized FOB is the right number for the model.

### 5. EUDR — **CONFIRMED (timeline as of 2026)**
- Coffee is in scope. Goods must be proven **deforestation-free (no deforestation after 31 Dec 2020)** with **plot-level geolocation coordinates** + legal-production evidence, via a Due Diligence Statement (DDS).
- **Application dates (post-delay):** large/medium operators **30 Dec 2026**; small/micro operators **30 Jun 2027**.
- Simplification: only the *first operator* placing product on the EU market must file a full DDS; Commission simplification review due 30 Apr 2026.
- **Implication for Finca Duna:** A new farm is *advantaged* here — capture GPS polygon boundaries of every plot from day one and document land-use history (proving no post-2020 deforestation). This is cheap to do early, expensive to retrofit, and becomes a *sales asset* (EU buyers will pay for clean, compliant, traceable lots).

### 6. Defensible price assumption — **ESTIMATED (built on confirmed anchors)**
Triangulating SCTG medians, Onyx EXW, and the Colombian Milds premium:
- **Conservative / base (sell through exporter, 84–86 pts):** **$3.40–$4.20/lb FOB.**
- **Target (direct-trade, 86–88 pts, organic + traceable):** **$4.20–$5.00/lb FOB.**
- **Upside (88+ / standout processing / named micro-lot):** **$5.00–$6.00+/lb FOB** (Wendelboe-class ~$6).
- **Recommended model assumption:** anchor the base case at **~$4.00/lb FOB**, with a clearly-flagged premium upside to ~$5/lb as quality reputation builds. Avoid modeling 88+ exotic pricing as the base — that is upside, not baseline.

---

## DECISION IT INFORMS
Feeds the **revenue model / Capital node**: green-coffee $/lb × yield × hectares = top-line. Use $4.00/lb FOB base, $5.00/lb stretch. Also informs go-to-market choice (exporter vs. direct trade) and the EUDR-compliance line item in the operating plan.

## PHASE
Phase 1 — market & pricing validation (pre-investment). Pricing assumption is now defensible enough to build the revenue model; refine with real buyer LOIs in Phase 2.

## OPEN UNKNOWNS / RISKS
- **Yield & quality realization:** price band assumes the farm actually hits 84–88 pt cup scores consistently — unproven for a new operation.
- **Nomad/Counter Culture per-lot $/kg** not captured (some pages 403-blocked); the SCTG medians and Onyx points are the firmer anchors.
- **Premium ≠ guaranteed:** SNSM baseline reputation is "clean/consistent," not "exotic"; the >$5 band requires deliberate processing/variety work.
- **Price volatility:** 2025 prices are at historic highs; the C-market and Colombian Milds premium can compress. Model should stress-test a down-cycle.
- **EUDR execution risk:** dates have slipped twice already; requirements could shift again, but geolocation capture is low-regret regardless.
- **Farmgate vs FOB gap:** ensure the model uses producer-realized FOB, not roaster landed cost.
