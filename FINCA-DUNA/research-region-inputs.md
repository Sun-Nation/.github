## 1. SOLAR — Global Solar Atlas (SNSM / Santa Marta region)

**Datapoints (regional):**
- 📊 **GHI (Global Horizontal Irradiation):** ~**4.8–5.5 kWh/m²/day** for the Magdalena / Santa Marta area (≈ **1,750–2,000 kWh/m²/yr**). [OPS Colombia atlas](https://www.opscolombia.com/blog/atlas-solar-colombia)
- 📊 **PVOUT (specific yield):** Colombia Caribbean region ≈ **1,500–1,560 kWh/kWp/yr** at the coast (performance ratio ~80%). National range ~975–1,750 kWh/kWp/yr; the Caribbean/Magdalena lowlands sit at the **upper end**. [World Bank / GSA PV potential study](https://globalsolaratlas.info/global-pv-potential-study), [ResearchGate – Caribbean Colombia PV assessment](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318119910_Assessment_of_the_energy_generation_potential_of_photovoltaic_systems_in_Caribbean_region_of_Colombia)
- 📊 **Daily energy yield:** ~**4.2–4.8 kWh/kWp/day** equivalent.
- ⚠️ Coffee zones at 1,000–1,900 m may differ from coastal/lowland values: higher elevation can mean slightly higher GHI but more **orographic cloud/afternoon convection** on windward slopes, which depresses real-world PVOUT. Mountain microclimate and slope aspect/shading matter a lot here — only a point query + on-site assessment resolves it.

**What it informs:** Sizing of (a) **solar coffee drying** (parabolic/solar dryers, dehumidification) and (b) **solar water pumping** for irrigation/wet-mill supply. At ~1,500 kWh/kWp/yr, off-grid PV is clearly viable; a few kWp can run pumps and supplement drying, reducing diesel/grid dependence — a real OPEX and ESG story for investors.

**Viability read:** ✅ Favourable. The region is solar-rich; the binding constraint is **cloud cover/seasonality on the slope**, not annual resource. Drying still needs covered/hybrid design because harvest can coincide with wetter months.

**Caveats:** 📊 Regional only — **needs exact coordinates** for a Global Solar Atlas point report (PVOUT, GHI, optimal tilt, monthly profile, horizon shading). Source resolution is ~1 km, which is coarse for steep terrain.

## 2. CLIMATE & WATER — IDEAM + CGIAR SNSM dataset

**Datapoints (regional):**
- 📊 **Rainfall — Magdalena flank:** ~**1,000 → 2,500+ mm/yr**; coffee belt commonly **1,200–2,700 mm/yr**. Higher SW elevations reach ~4,000–4,100 mm/yr. [Wikipedia – SNSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_de_Santa_Marta), [Barista Hustle – SNSM](https://www.baristahustle.com/lesson/cbgc-1-19-sierra-nevada-de-santa-marta/)
- 📊 **Rainfall — La Guajira flank:** much drier, **~300–1,500 mm/yr** (driest in Colombia). The flank/aspect of the farm massively changes the water budget. [ScienceDirect – SNSM cacao water sufficiency](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214581822002683)
- 📊 **Seasonality:** **bimodal** rainfall typical of northern Colombia — two wet peaks (roughly Apr–Jun and Sep–Nov) with a mid-year and a Dec–Mar dry spell; the long dry season aligns with main harvest/drying (helpful for drying, a risk for water supply).
- 📊 **Temperature:** coffee-belt elevations ~**18–24 °C** mean; páramo summits ~5–6 °C. Cooler nights at altitude aid bean density / cup quality.
- ✅ **Reference dataset (CGIAR/Alliance Bioversity–CIAT):** *Post-processed climate dataset for the SNSM Region* — monthly precipitation, Tmax, Tmin **and potential evapotranspiration (PET)**, gap-filled from **IDEAM** daily data, **1989–2018 (30 yr)**, **1 km** grid, CSV + GeoTIFF. Built from 10 precip + 5 temperature IDEAM stations within 15 km. [CGIAR publication page](https://www.cgiar.org/research/publication/post-processed-climate-dataset-sierra-nevada-de-santa-marta-snsm-region-colombia/)
  - ⚠️ Same dataset's cacao study found rainfall insufficient to prevent yield loss on **10 of 27 farms** — i.e. **water deficits are real in parts of the massif**, especially the drier flanks. Strong signal that a water/irrigation plan is not optional.

**What it informs:** Whether the farm needs **supplemental irrigation/water storage**, the **drying strategy** (sun vs. covered/mechanical), and processing capacity (wet milling is water-intensive — eco-pulpers / water recirculation may be needed where rainfall is marginal).

**Investor read:** Magdalena-flank coffee elevations have **adequate-to-generous rainfall** with a dry season favourable for harvest/drying — supportive. The decisive variable is **which flank/elevation the farm is on**: Guajira-side or rain-shadow positions could be water-constrained.

**Caveats:** 📊 Regional ranges; **needs exact coordinates** to extract the farm's own monthly precip/PET from the CGIAR 1-km grid and the nearest IDEAM station record. 🧪 On-site rain gauge + spring/stream flow measurement recommended for ≥1 cycle.

## 3. SOIL — ISRIC SoilGrids + IGAC

**Datapoints (regional, model-based):**
- 📊 **Soil type:** volcanic-influenced mountain soils; coffee zones of the Colombian Andes/SNSM commonly include **Andisols** (and Inceptisols), valued for high organic matter, good structure and water retention. [Springer – SOC Andean Colombia](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10661-025-14123-1)
- 📊 **Soil Organic Carbon (SOC):** Andisols on cool, steep Colombian slopes run high — study range **~20–294 t/ha** (0–100 cm), some Andean forest profiles ~430 Mg C/ha. Expect SNSM coffee soils to be **organically rich** but variable. [NCBI – Colombian Andean SOC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12290775/)
- 📊 **pH:** Andean coffee soils are typically **acidic, ~pH 4.5–5.5** (Andisols tend acidic; coffee tolerates/prefers ~5.0–5.5). Confirm on-site — drives lime/amendment plan.
- 📊 **Texture:** generally **loam to clay-loam / silt-loam**, free-draining on volcanic parent material. SoilGrids gives % sand/silt/clay, bulk density, CEC, N at 7 depths. [ISRIC SoilGrids](https://isric.org/explore/soilgrids), [SoilGrids250m – PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0169748)
- 📊 **National legacy data:** **IGAC** (Instituto Geográfico Agustín Codazzi) holds Colombia's official soil surveys; commonly combined with SoilGrids to fill bulk-density/coarse-fragment gaps.

**What it informs:** Whether soils support **regenerative specialty coffee** without heavy remediation — fertility (SOC/N/CEC), **liming need** (pH), drainage/erosion risk on slopes, and the carbon-stock baseline for any **soil-carbon / regenerative narrative**.

**Investor read:** Likely **favourable** — the SNSM/Andean profile (rich Andisols, high SOC, good structure) is exactly what premium coffee wants. But this is the area where **desk data is weakest**.

**Caveats:** 🧪 **Must be confirmed by on-site soil tests** (lab pH, SOC, N-P-K, CEC, texture, micronutrients across the actual blocks). SoilGrids is a 250 m statistical model — unreliable on steep heterogeneous terrain. 📊 **Needs exact coordinates** for even a meaningful SoilGrids/IGAC point pull.

## OPEN UNKNOWNS / RISKS
1. **EXACT FARM COORDINATES (blocker for all four sections).** Without the precise point we cannot pull farm-specific PVOUT/GHI, monthly precip/PET, nearest-IDEAM record, or SoilGrids/IGAC values. **Top priority to obtain.**
2. **Which flank & elevation.** Magdalena/windward (wetter, 1,200–2,700 mm) vs. La Guajira/rain-shadow (300–1,500 mm) flips the entire water story. Material to viability.
3. **Water security.** CGIAR cacao study shows real deficits on part of the massif → may require storage/irrigation + water-efficient processing. Quantify with on-site flow/rain data over ≥1 cycle. 🧪
4. **Soil reality vs. model.** SoilGrids/IGAC are coarse on steep terrain; actual fertility, pH (liming need), drainage and erosion risk must come from **on-site lab tests**. 🧪
5. **Solar microclimate.** Orographic cloud/afternoon convection on slopes can cut real PVOUT below lowland figures; drying may need hybrid/covered design. 🧪
6. **Ecotourism figures are directional.** Vendor market sizes diverge 1.5×; Colombia press numbers are promotional. Replace with bottom-up local capacity/pricing model before any revenue line.
7. **Access/security/indigenous land considerations** in the SNSM (not researched here) — flag for due diligence before tourism build-out.
